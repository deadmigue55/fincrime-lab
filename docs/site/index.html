FinCrime Lab

Fraud, anti-money-laundering, experiments and fraud loss on a simulated Mexican neobank

WarningEverything here is synthetic

The bank is a simulator (Banco Sintético), not a real institution. Methods are tested against planted truth, so findings about method transfer and findings about level (how much fraud is caught, what capital is) do not. External checks on public benchmarks are labelled as such.

Headline results

Module Question Result
Fraud scoring Rules or model, at the same review workload? At 0.54% of attempts reviewed the model finds 99.3% of fraud events against 40.4% for the rules; on the hard BAF benchmark it finds 54.7% at 5% false positives.
Fraud loss and capital How many claims are still to come, and what capital? Capital MXN 2.12M (90% interval 1.14 to 3.77); the 90% forecast interval covered the realised loss in 92% of 200 banks.
Experiments How wrong is an A/B test of a fraud rule? Checking weekly gives a false winner in 28% of A/A tests; alpha-spending holds 3% to 3%. Fraud loss is underpowered at 100,000 customers per arm.
AML Rules, trees or graph, and how many cases a week? Rules find 100% of laundering episodes at 14.8 cases a week; trees find 90% against 66% at 5 cases; graph features are unstable here but lift AMLworld PR-AUC from 0.13 to 0.41.

Each result has a protocol written before the models were scored (steps 6 to 8; step 9 is attested in text only), a read-only code review by a second reader (steps 7 to 9, not an independent validation), and a limit stated next to it. Two checks fell short and are reported as such: the takeover-loss A/A rate (4.0% against a 4.1% band edge) and detection of an outcome-dependent sample loss (83.9%). The AML comparison at 20 cases a week is saturated and cannot rank detectors.
