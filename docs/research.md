context. Written 2026-10-22 before any code. Every claim is tagged:

## 3. Mexican context, for calibrating the synthetic bank

No Mexican transaction-level data is public. The generator (step 2) is therefore calibrated to
published aggregates. Numbers below are the anchors and their confidence.

| Anchor | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| SPEI transfers in 2025 | > 7,300 million, +36.8% vs 2024, said by Banxico's governor to the Senate | **[checked]**, press report of the Senate statement |
| SPEI amount in 2025 | ~600 trillion pesos (STP), mean ~82,000 pesos per transfer (our division) | **[to verify]**. Not a retail anchor: the mean is dominated by large interbank and corporate transfers. Retail amounts are calibrated on the share under 10,000 pesos instead |
| SPEI share under 10,000 pesos | 62% of operations in 2014, 94% in 2025 | **[to verify]** opinion article citing Banxico |
| Default user transfer limit (MTU) | 12,500 pesos if the customer sets none | **[to verify]**, year unclear |

| Account opened via internet or a non-bank app | 10.3% of adults in 2024 (2.7% in 2021) | **[checked]** same release, chart 3 |
| Account holders using a phone app | 69.1% (54.3% in 2021); ATM use fell 16.8 points | **[checked]** same release |

| Population by state | 32 states, total 126,014,024 (2020 census) | **[checked]** INEGI via Wikipedia table |

| Fraud claims, Jan-Sep 2025 | 3.82M claims (72% of all), MXN 16,678M | **[to verify]** attributed to the Buro de Entidades Financieras |
| Mule accounts blocked 2014-2024 | 44,899 accounts of 6,941 people (UIF) | **[checked]** press report |
| CNBV fraud-prevention rules | in force 2024 with later delays; per-customer transaction limit; typology and information-sharing duties | **[to verify]** dates conflict between sources |
| UIF typology structure | method, technique, instrument, case, alert signal, trend | **[checked]** |

**Limits of these anchors.** CONDUSEF figures count *claims* (reclamaciones), not confirmed fraud,
and about three quarters of the claimed amount is not refunded, so they are neither a fraud rate
nor a loss figure. We also have no denominator (transactions or active accounts of a comparable
institution), so a per-transaction fraud rate cannot be derived from them. The synthetic bank's
fraud prevalence is therefore a **named assumption**, chosen for the evaluation to be meaningful
(see `methodology.md`), and the CONDUSEF numbers are used only for order of magnitude and for the
share of fraud by channel once a primary table is found.

Things we did not find and must not invent: the reporting thresholds for unusual and relevant
operations, the national risk assessment, fraud losses by channel for 2025. Where the generator
needs a number we cannot source, it is a named parameter in `docs/assumptions.md`, marked as an
assumption, and the sensitivity of every result to it is reported.

### What loading the benchmarks showed (step 3)

- **AMLworld Small, checked on the loaded files.** HI-Small has 5,078,345 transactions of which 5,177
  are labelled laundering (0.102%); LI-Small has 6,924,049 and 3,565 (0.0515%). The data spans only
  17 to 18 days (2022-09-01 onwards). This settles the 5.1K versus 3.6K disagreement between sources.
- **Eight pattern types** in the `Patterns.txt` files: fan-in, fan-out, cycle, stack, bipartite,
  scatter-gather, gather-scatter and random.
- **The patterns file is not complete ground truth.** HI-Small lists 3,209 transactions, every one
  present in the main file, but 5,177 are labelled laundering: about 38% of laundering transactions
  belong to no listed scheme. Coverage is 62% (HI-Small) and 65% (HI-Medium) but only 29% (LI-Small) and
  24% (LI-Medium). Typology-coverage numbers must say which denominator they use.
- **Sizes on disk.** HI-Medium has 31,898,238 transactions (35,230 laundering, 0.110%) and LI-Medium
  31,251,483 (16,041, 0.051%), spanning 27 to 28 days. Full table: `docs/benchmarks.md`.
- **Simulator artefact to keep in mind.** In HI-Small, ACH transfers carry a 0.75% laundering rate
  against about 0.02% for cheque, card, cash, wire and bitcoin, so payment format alone is a strong
  signal that a real bank would not have. Results on AMLworld should be shown with and without it.
- **BAF is balanced across variants in prevalence only.** All six files have the same overall fraud
  rate (1.103%); the variants differ in group sizes and in prevalence by group.

### Licences and what may be published

- Raw data of every dataset stays out of the repo and out of any public demo bundle.
- AMLworld is CDLA-Sharing-1.0: derived *data* published must carry the same licence. We publish
  only aggregate results and figures, plus the code that builds them.
- BAF and IEEE-CIS terms are not confirmed; same rule, results only, until read at step 3.
- The synthetic bank is our own work and its sample can be published (it describes no real person).
- Mexican identifiers (CURP, RFC, CLABE) are generated with the right *format* but random content and a
  deliberately invalid check digit, so none can belong to a real person.

## 4. Pitfalls that separate production work from Kaggle work

- Chargeback labels arrive 30-120 days late **[checked, practitioner guide]**, so the newest data
  is the least labelled. Models trained without accounting for it miss new patterns. **[checked]**
- Declined transactions produce no label, so a model only ever learns from what it allowed
  (selection bias, "reject inference"). **[checked]**
- Compare models on cohorts with equal label maturity, at the operating threshold the business uses. **[checked]**

## 5. Where the author's actuarial background gives an edge

- Chargeback maturation is the same problem as claims lag (IBNR): completion factors by report lag.
- Fraud loss = frequency x severity: compound loss distributions, capital, stop-loss, as in the
  reinsurance step of the Mortality Table Builder.
- Credibility weighting for small segments, planted-effect validation of methods (the experience
  study recovered 15 of 15 planted effects), and the habit of documenting every assumption.
